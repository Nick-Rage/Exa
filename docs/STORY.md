# The story — one sentence, then the day

**Google gives Maya pages to read. Exa gives her a shortlist already checked against her rules — with citations.**

That is the whole pitch. Everything else is proof.

---

## Who

**Maya Chen** — Capture Manager, Public Sector, Meridian Identity.  
Sells cloud IAM. Thursday 10am is bid/no-bid. She owns the shortlist.

## What her day looks like without Exa

3–4 hours every Monday:

1. Paste keywords into Google + SAM.gov  
2. Open 20–40 tabs and PDFs  
3. For each one ask: still open? identity? hardware? can we prime?  
4. Paste survivors into a spreadsheet  

**The failure:** keyword search cannot answer those questions. It only ranks pages. **Maya is the research engine.**

## What changes with Exa

~15 minutes of human review:

1. Exa researches the same intent across the open web  
2. Opens the notices and judges her five bid rules  
3. Returns **records with evidence** — not blue links  
4. Maya decides Pursue / Watch / Pass  

**The win:** she stops being the PDF reader. She stays the decision-maker.

## Why Exa over Google (say this)

| | Regular search | Exa Agent |
|---|---|---|
| Output | Links | Judged records |
| Open / closed? | You check | Gate + evidence |
| Identity vs door hardware? | You check | Gate + evidence |
| Can we prime? | You check | Gate + evidence |
| Next Monday | Same hunt again | “What’s new” (exclusions) |
| Consumer | A person browsing | A workflow / shortlist |

Google is built for a human to browse. Exa is built for software to research and return structured, cited answers into a day-to-day job.

## What you are selling in the interview

Not “BidSignal the company.”

You are selling: **I can put Exa inside a real worker’s Monday so they feel the difference vs Google in one screen.**
